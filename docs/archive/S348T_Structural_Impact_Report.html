3. Binding Affinity & Steric Analysis

While global stability is high, the local introduction of a methyl group significantly alters the S1 binding pocket dynamics.

The "Methyl Clash" Mechanism

The mutation of Serine (small hydroxymethyl) to Threonine (branched methyl group) introduces steric bulk at position 348. This addition directly impacts the central binding pocket where ligands like Escitalopram reside.

Comparison to S438T: In analogous systems, the introduction of a single methyl group into the binding pocket has been shown to result in up to a 175-fold decrease in affinity (increase in Ki) due to steric interference with the ligand's dimethylaminopropyl chain.
  • Ligand Positioning: Escitalopram is positioned centrally; the S348T mutation likely forces a repositioning of the ligand to avoid the new γ-methyl group [cite: analysis_report.html].
  • Van der Waals Disruption: The change subtly alters the local VdW environment, potentially disrupting the optimal seating of the inhibitor [cite: analysis_report.html].
