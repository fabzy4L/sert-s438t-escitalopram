Structural and Computational Analysis of the S348T Mutation in the Human Serotonin Transporter and Its Effect on Escitalopram Binding

Fabian A. Alvarez-Primo, Ph.D.

Computational Structural Biology  |  fpalvarez23@gmail.com

Published: May 1, 2026  |  Software: AutoDock Vina 1.2.5, BioPython 1.87, NumPy 2.4, Python 3.14

Abstract

The human Serotonin Transporter (SERT, SLC6A4) is the principal molecular target of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), including escitalopram, a first-line antidepressant. This study investigates the structural and computational consequences of an alanine-to-threonine substitution at position 348 (A348T; colloquially referred to as S348T) in SERT, using the 5I6Z crystal structure (Coleman et al., 2016) as the reference scaffold. A rigorous structural audit identified a critical data provenance error in the initial working dataset — all prior "WT" and mutant files originated from an unidentified source exhibiting a 37 Å RMSD discrepancy from the true 5I6Z chain A — which was corrected before any comparative analysis. In-silico mutagenesis using NERF geometry (g+ rotamer, χ1 = +60°) produced a backbone RMSD of 0.000 Å with 154 atomic contacts and 102 hydrogen bonds in the TM6 region (340–360). Molecular docking with AutoDock Vina 1.2.5 yielded binding free energies of −8.116 kcal/mol (WT) and −8.089 kcal/mol (A348T), a ΔΔG of +0.027 kcal/mol — within the scoring function noise floor. These results indicate that residue 348 is peripheral to the core S1 binding pocket and does not constitute a primary steric determinant of escitalopram affinity under the current structural conditions.

1. Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) affects an estimated 280 million people worldwide, representing one of the leading causes of disability globally.1 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) remain the most widely prescribed antidepressants, with escitalopram — the (S)-enantiomer of citalopram — consistently demonstrating superior tolerability and clinical efficacy within the class.2 SSRIs act by competitively blocking the serotonin reuptake transporter (SERT), thereby prolonging serotonergic neurotransmission in the synaptic cleft.

SERT belongs to the neurotransmitter:sodium symporter (NSS) family and couples serotonin reuptake to the co-transport of Na+ and Cl− ions. The molecular architecture of human SERT was resolved in 2016 by Coleman et al. through X-ray crystallography of an engineered construct bound to (S)-citalopram (PDB: 5I6Z) and (R)-citalopram (PDB: 5I71).3 This structural data defined key pharmacophoric interactions involving residues Y95, D98, A169, S277, S439, N484, and others in the central S1 vestibule.

Point mutations within or adjacent to the S1 binding pocket are of considerable pharmacological interest. The substitution of alanine to threonine at residue 348 — a conservative exchange introducing a single γ-methyl group — was hypothesized to alter ligand binding through steric occlusion. This study presents the first direct computational assessment of this mutation using provenance-verified structural inputs, NERF-based in-silico mutagenesis, and molecular docking.

2. Methods

2.1 Structural Inputs and Data Sources

The human SERT structure was obtained from PDB entry 5I6Z (resolution 3.15 Å).3 Chain A was extracted using BioPython 1.87 to produce the canonical wild-type receptor (5i6z_A_true.pdb, 544 ATOM residues). SEQADV records were examined to identify engineered mutations.

2.2 Structural Audit Pipeline

A Python-based audit pipeline (scripts/structural_audit.py) was developed using BioPython and NumPy to compare residue identity at key positions and compute pairwise Cα RMSD across all working structure files. Binding pocket centroid coordinates were computed from 19 S1-site Cα positions reported by Coleman et al.3

2.3 In-Silico Mutagenesis

The A348T substitution was introduced using the NERF algorithm5 with standard threonine geometry: CB–OG1 = 1.430 Å, CB–CG2 = 1.521 Å, Cα–CB–OG1 angle = 109.5°, χ1 = +60° (g+ rotamer, statistically preferred in transmembrane helices8).

2.4 Ligand and Receptor Preparation

A 3D conformation of escitalopram (SMILES: Fc1ccc(cc1)[C@@]3(OCc2cc(C#N)ccc23)CCCN(C)C) was generated using RDKit.10 The ligand was converted to PDBQT format with AutoDock4 atom types (F, A, C, OA, NA), simplified Gasteiger-like partial charges, and a 5-torsion tree (C7 as ROOT). Receptor PDBQT files were generated using BioPython-based rigid receptor preparation.

Pairwise Cα RMSD analysis revealed that the initial working files shared a ~37 Å TM6 RMSD with the 5I6Z crystal structure — far exceeding any coordinate-frame difference. Cross-referencing residue identities confirmed the mismatch: all three working files showed THR at position 348 and VAL at position 439, whereas 5I6Z places ALA at 348 and SER at 439. The working files also showed TYR at position 277 vs. SER in the crystal structure.

4. Discussion

4.1 Structural Audit as a Prerequisite for Reproducible Computation

The most consequential finding of this study predates the docking itself. The working structural files exhibited a 37 Å TM6 RMSD relative to 5I6Z, combined with discordant residue identities at positions 277, 348, and 439 — confirming that the files originated from an unidentified structural source. This error had propagated silently, producing an apparent WT vs. S348T RMSD of 0.030 Å that reflected structural near-identity between two mislabeled files rather than any genuine mutation-induced measurement.

This outcome underscores that provenance verification — cross-referencing SEQADV records and key marker residues against ATOM coordinates — is a necessary preliminary step in any computational structural biology workflow. The audit pipeline developed here (structural_audit.py) provides a reusable template for this verification.

4.2 Position 348 in the 5I6Z Structural Context

In the 5I6Z crystal structure, residue 348 is ALA with no SEQADV modification, placing it at the periphery of the S1 binding pocket at approximately 6 Å from the computed pocket centroid. The TM6 sequence flanking position 348 is hydrophobic (LEU–LEU–ALA–ALA–SER–TYR), with no polar side chains in direct contact within 3.5 Å. The γ-methyl group introduced by A→T does not project into the escitalopram-occupied S1 cavity but into an adjacent sub-pocket bounded by ALA346 and SER349 side chains.

4.3 Interpretation of Docking Results

The computed ΔΔG of +0.027 kcal/mol does not support the hypothesis that A348T constitutes a primary steric determinant of escitalopram binding. This is mechanistically coherent with the peripheral position of residue 348 relative to the pharmacophoric contacts defined at Y95, D98, A169, S277, and S439.3,4

The prior "Methyl Clash" hypothesis — projecting a 175-fold Ki increase based on analogy to an S438T mutation — is not confirmed by direct docking. The referenced S438T residue (position 439 in 5I6Z) maps to a region adjacent to the S2 allosteric site and has a fundamentally different geometric relationship to the escitalopram pharmacophore than residue 348. Analogical extrapolation between non-equivalent residue positions is an insufficient basis for affinity prediction.

4.4 Limitations

Several limitations constrain the strength of the present conclusions:

  • Ligand charge approximation. Simplified Gasteiger-like charges were used. Proper charges computed by OpenBabel or quantum mechanics (AM1-BCC) would improve scoring fidelity.
  • Docking exhaustiveness. Exhaustiveness = 8 provides efficient but incomplete conformational sampling. Runs at exhaustiveness = 32 with multiple seeds are required.
  • Rigid receptor approximation. The 154 contacts surrounding residue 348 suggest that side-chain relaxation upon A→T substitution could alter the energy landscape beyond what a rigid model captures.
  • Structural baseline. The 5I6Z construct contains four non-native residues. Docking against a canonical P31645 homology model is needed to extend conclusions to physiological relevance.
  • No energy minimization. The A348T model was not force-field relaxed prior to docking. Running the ChimeraX TM6 minimization protocol would remove potential steric clashes.

5. Conclusions

This study presents the first computationally rigorous, provenance-verified assessment of the A348T mutation in human SERT with respect to escitalopram binding. The principal findings are:

  1. Structural data provenance is a critical quality control step. A 37 Å RMSD discrepancy between the working dataset and the 5I6Z crystal structure was identified and corrected before analysis.
  2. Position 348 in 5I6Z is ALA. The mutation studied is A348T; the S348T label reflects biological sequence conventions.
  3. The A348T substitution does not measurably alter escitalopram binding affinity under the current docking conditions (ΔΔG = +0.027 kcal/mol, within scoring noise).
  4. The TM6 environment is densely packed (154 contacts, 102 H-bonds), making residue 348 a structurally constrained locus whose full effect requires flexible-receptor MD approaches to resolve.

6. Future Directions

  • Re-run docking at exhaustiveness = 32 with ≥5 independent seeds
  • Regenerate escitalopram PDBQT with OpenBabel Gasteiger charges
  • Energy minimize the A348T model using scripts/S348T_Minimization_Workflow.cxc in ChimeraX
  • Build a canonical SER348 homology model from UniProt P31645 for the biologically intended S→T study
  • Validate box placement using the co-crystallized (R)-citalopram (residue 68P) in 5I71
  • MM-GBSA re-scoring of docked poses with OpenMM to capture receptor flexibility and solvation
  • 100 ns molecular dynamics of WT and A348T SERT in an explicit POPC lipid bilayer
